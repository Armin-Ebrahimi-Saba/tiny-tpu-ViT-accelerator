Depth on tiny-tpu

Depth Anything V2 Small, the whole network, on a Nexys Video FPGA. The PC only prepares the picture and reads the answer back. Everything between runs on the board, every result is byte-identical to the Python emulator, and it takes 7.4 s per 126×126 picture.

Who moves which bytes

Time runs downward. Each vertical line is a place on the board or the PC; an arrow is data moving between two of them, coloured by what drives the move. The program is loaded once. After that, a picture costs the 48.5 KB input in, one pass over the descriptor list, and the 254 KB depth map out.

Where the 7.2 seconds go

Measured on the board with mcycle at 50 MHz, per picture. The encoder is limited by the DMA; the head by the CPU building im2col rows one 32-bit word at a time.

{{BARS}}

Where things live

Program
26.6 MB in DDR3 at 0x8000_0000: 7,717 descriptors, int8 weights, per-channel constants
Arena
13.8 MB directly above the program: every activation, the input, the depth map
On-chip buffers
ACT 8 KB, WGT 48 KB, OUT 8 KB, plus the config region; 115 BRAM tiles
Driver
tpu_runtime.c on the CV32E40P, from BRAM; it interprets descriptors and decides nothing
Decisions
sw/export_tpu.py on the PC: tiling, layouts, scales from 5 calibration photos
Link
USB JTAG through OpenOCD, about 284 KiB/s: 91 s for the program, once
